What it does

CiteExtract is designed to verify citations in academic writing. For each reference, it considers two questions separately: whether the cited work exists with the stated bibliographic details, and whether it supports the claim made in the text. Each assessment is supported by evidence, such as database records for the bibliographic check and relevant excerpts from the cited work for the claim check.

Authors can use it to review their manuscripts before submission, while reviewers can use it to examine references more efficiently. The tool also produces an annotated PDF in which citations are labeled with their assessment, and any disputed claims are flagged with notes that point to the relevant supporting passages.

Verdict guide

Metadata verdicts describe the cited paper itself.

  • VALID. The paper exists and its fields match.
  • FABRICATED. At least two databases responded with no match, the metadata mismatches a real paper, or the paper has been retracted. Suggested action: remove the citation.
  • UNVERIFIABLE. Coverage was insufficient to decide. Only one database responded, or all timed out. Flagged for human review.

Claim verdicts describe the relationship between the cited paper and the citing sentence.

  • SUPPORTS. The cited paper says what the citing sentence attributes to it.
  • CONTRADICTS. The cited paper says something materially different. Suggested action: re-read the cited paper and revise.
  • NEUTRAL. The cited paper does not address the specific topic the citing sentence claims. The general area may overlap but the specific point is not entailed.
  • UNVERIFIABLE. Claim verification could not run, for example because the full text is paywalled, the agent failed, or there is no substantive citing sentence.

Frequently asked questions

Which databases does the tool consult?

The tool consults CrossRef, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, PubMed, and arXiv. For non-paper citations such as tweets or blog posts, it also checks whether the cited URL resolves.

What model is used for claim verification?

OpenAI's gpt-4o-mini.

Is my paper sent to OpenAI?

No. Only short excerpts are sent to OpenAI: the citing sentence with its surrounding context, and passages retrieved from the cited paper. Your full draft is never uploaded.

How accurate is it, and how should I read a verdict?

The tool's primary output is the evidence trail, not the verdict label. Every verdict shows you what was retrieved — which databases responded for an existence check, and which passages from the cited paper supported or contradicted each claim — and the report exposes all of it so you can read the underlying material and decide whether you agree.
